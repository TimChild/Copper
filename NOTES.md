Settings is fast again: the rebuilt panel no longer lags when you scroll, hover, type or switch pages. 1Password works in Copper, natively: unlock with Touch ID through the 1Password app (or your account password, or a service account token), and your 1Password logins, codes, cards and addresses fill like Bitwarden's; new passwords can be saved to 1Password, and agents can sign in with items you share. Settings is rebuilt — larger, grouped and clearer — with a search that finds any setting as you type, typos and other names included. Canvas invites show where they stand. In a canvas's Share sheet, each person you invited reads Invited, with Resend to remind them and × to take the invite back. Invites waiting for you are listed under Invitations in the Canvas card, and the invite pill now shows over Settings and the other panels; its × means Not now, and the invite stays listed. An invite you already took up another way, such as through the canvas's share link, no longer sticks on screen. History sync no longer gets stuck on one oversized page. Downloads work from Gmail and other sites that fetch a file in a hidden frame, and pages no longer leave stray or empty files in Downloads.

You can always see who is driving. The pill in the page's corner, the ring around it, the on-page trail and the timeline beside the page (⌥⌘J, now the Driver Timeline) that used to show only Jev runs now show every agent: phi or Claude Code calling browser tools over the loopback server, a bot through an agent link, the agent in Copper's own pane — one row per call in plain words, the latest reason the agent gave, and a Stop that takes the browser back (its next calls are refused, and it is told why). Bitwarden sign-in now takes email two-step codes and new-device verification codes: Copper runs bw login with its prompts on and asks you for the code as a second step, in Settings or through `copper bitwarden login`.

The first version. A browser for the Mac with nothing in the way: tabs in a row or down the side, pinned tabs that keep their place, and one field for addresses and searches. Ads are blocked before they load; passwords and passkeys stay in your keychain; anything on a page can be hidden; articles open in a reading mode and videos float. Chrome extensions install from the Chrome Web Store on macOS 15.4 or later — its button says Add to Copper. Tabs you haven't looked at for half an hour sleep and give their memory back. It runs on the engine already in macOS and weighs 2.9 MB.
