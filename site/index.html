A browser with nothing in the way.

Copper is a small, fast, quiet web browser for the Mac. WebKit underneath, about 5 MB, opens instantly.

    Quiet by default.

    No toolbar, no start page, no sidebar of suggestions, no account to sign into. The things people install extensions for are already here, each one keystroke away.

    • One field

      ⌘L

      Type an address and you go there; type words and you search. Nothing you type is sent anywhere until you press Return.

    • Tabs that stay out of the way

      ⇧⌘S

      Across the top or down the left. Pinned tabs shrink to a letter, and last session's tabs cost nothing until you click them.

    • Spaces

      ⌃N

      Rows of tabs with a name, an icon and a colour the whole column wears, and optionally a profile of their own.

    • Find any tab

      ⌘K

      Lists your open tabs by name as you type. The one you are on is always unmistakable.

    • Hide anything, for good

      ⇧⌘H

      Click a cookie banner or a newsletter overlay away. It stays gone on that site next time, before the page draws a frame.

    • Reading mode

      ⇧⌘R

      Strips a page down to the article.

    • An ad blocker that runs before the page

      Trackers and ad networks are stopped at the network level, so there is nothing to render and nothing to slow you down. On by default, off per site.

    • Video that follows you

      ⇧⌘P

      Lifts the video into a small window that stays above everything, including other apps.

    • Passwords, in your keychain

      ⌥⌘L

      Saved once a sign-in has actually worked, offered when you click the field, never filled on its own. Encrypted by macOS.

    • Updates itself, quietly

      A new build is downloaded and verified in the background first. Copper never restarts without your say-so. Light, dark, or the Mac's own — the frame and the pages follow.

    Moves in from Chrome or Arc.

    Flow is one button. It reads the other browser's files on your Mac and never changes them. Imported pages stay asleep until you visit them, and macOS asks once before handing over the other browser's key.

    Chrome extensions, without Chrome

    Paste a Chrome Web Store link, or press Add on the extension's page. It runs on WebKit's own extension engine, the one Safari uses, and Copper fills in the Chrome APIs WebKit lacks. macOS 15.4 or later.

    Built for agents.

    Copper is an MCP server. Turn it on and Claude Code, phi or any other MCP client gets the window you already have open, with your tabs and your sign-ins, through the tool names Playwright MCP uses.

    • One switch. Settings › Agents › Let agents drive this window. Set up for Claude Code and phi is one press, no token pasted into a prompt.
    • A copper command for the shell, linked into your PATH by Homebrew. It never starts Copper on its own.
    • Loopback only, behind a bearer token readable by you alone. Pages in other browsers can't reach in.

    On your Mac, and nowhere else.

    The only things that leave your Mac are the pages you ask for, their icons, and one small request a day to see whether there is a newer version.

    No accountNothing to sign into, nothing to sync, no cloud.
    No telemetryNo analytics, and no crash reports sent anywhere.
    No serverYour tabs, history and passwords stay on the Mac.
    Where everything is
    WhatWhere it isWho can read it
    PasswordsThe macOS login keychain, as ordinary keychain items tagged CopperCopper. Any other app triggers the system's permission dialog.
    History, bookmarks, open tabs, hidden elementsSmall JSON files in ~/Library/Application Support/Copper/You.
    Cookies and site dataWebKit's own store for the appThe sites that set them, as in any browser.
    ExtensionsUnpacked in Copper's own folder, their data in WebKit's extension storeEach extension, within the permissions you accepted.
    Anything elseNowhere. There is no server.No one.

    A private tab (⇧⌘N) has its own cookie jar and leaves nothing behind when it closes.

    Install.

    For macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple Silicon. Pick one; after that Copper updates itself.

    Homebrew Recommended

    brew install --cask copper-browser/copper/copper

    Also links the copper command into your PATH.

    One-line installer

    curl -fsSL https://github.com/copper-browser/Copper/releases/latest/download/copper-install.sh | sh

    The same build, without Homebrew.

    Download the app

    Download for macOS

    Unzip it and drag Copper.app to Applications.

    About the first launch. Copper builds are ad-hoc signed, not notarized. Homebrew and the installer clear the quarantine flag for you. After a manual download, run this once, or right-click Copper and choose Open:

    xattr -cr /Applications/Copper.app
